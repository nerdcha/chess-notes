Endgame Reflexes (Starter)

Why this exists: at 2+1, games actually reach endgames (at 1+0 they mostly didn't — the very first recorded game lost a winning position on time). This is a seed note, to grow from real games the same way the opening reflex notes did.

The One-Sentence Compass

Queens off? King on. Then push the passer.

Reflex 1: Activate the King

The moment queens leave the board, the king stops being a liability and becomes your strongest minor piece. Walk it toward the center / the action.

Mistake it prevents: shuffling rooks on the back rank while the opponent's king eats your pawns.

Reflex 2: Passed Pawns Must Be Pushed

If you have a passed pawn, pushing it is the default move whenever nothing is hanging. Put rooks BEHIND passed pawns (yours or theirs).

The picture — the circled rook shoves from behind and gains scope with every push; Black's rook in front loses a square each time:

At 2+1 this is also clock strategy: pushing a passer is fast, safe, and forces THEM to burn time.

Reflex 3: Up Material? Trade Pieces, Not Pawns

  • Winning: trade pieces (simplify toward an easy K+P win), keep pawns (you need something to promote).
  • Losing: the reverse — trade pawns, keep pieces, hunt for tricks.

Memory trick: Ahead: pieces off. Behind: pawns off.

Reflex 4: King and Pawn — Get In Front

In K+P vs K: your king belongs IN FRONT of the pawn, not behind it. If your king reaches the 6th rank in front of the pawn, it's always winning. Escort with the king, don't race the pawn ahead alone.

Reflex 5: Stalemate Check

When they're down to a bare king (or nearly), before every non-check move ask one question: does he have a move? Cheap draws from stalemate are the classic sub-1200 heartbreak.
