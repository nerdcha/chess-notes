Pregame Card

Thirty seconds before you play. One compass per opening, the reflexes that have cost you games, and the last lesson. Nothing else.

Every move, half a second

  • A piece just arrived in your half? First ask whether it is simply hanging.
  • A piece just left a square? Trace the line it stopped blocking.
  • Check first, collect second. Qa4+ before any automatic recapture.
  • Before landing a piece, name what attacks that square. Count attackers one move away too.

Botvinnik (White)

Compass: Fianchetto first, then the centre. Setup done? h3, Kh2, then f4-f5.

  • 1...d5: cxd5, just take. After 2...c6 or 2...e6, take again.
  • 1...e5 2.Nc3 Nf6 3.g3 d5 (one game in five): cxd5 before Bg2. If they ever hold your c-pawn on c4 with their king on e8: Qa4+ first, collect second.
  • ...Bg4 pinning Nge2 with a knight on d4 or about to land there: f3, not h3.
  • You played a3+b4 and ...a5 appears: move the knight (Na4) before any setup move.
  • The pawns in front of your tucked king are bodyguards. Push them to attack, never to grab.
  • A ...c6/...d5 duo hits c4 and e4: resolve it yourself with cxd5.
  • Before any trade, ask whose piece is better. A knight on d5 stays on d5.
  • 1...c6 2.Nc3 d5 3.cxd5 cxd5: 4.d4 before g3, or ...d4 kicks your knight. Later, if ...e5 leaves d5 isolated: count attackers and take it.

Caro-Kann (Black vs 1.e4)

Compass: Take, push or defend: each has one plan. Bishop out before ...e6; vs the push, everything hits d4.

  • 3.e5: ...c5! then ...Nc6. Vs Nf3: ...cxd4 first, then ...Bg4 and take on f3. Vs dxc5: ...e6, ...Bxc5.
  • 2.e5: ...d5. 3.d4 is the Advance (...c5); 3.exd6 ...exd6, then ...d5 and ...Re8.
  • 3.exd5: ...cxd5, then ...Bg4 pins the d4 defender, ...Rc8 every game, then ...b5-b4.
  • 3.Nc3 or 3.Nd2: ...dxe4, ...Nf6, ...exf6. Castle, then Bg4. Knight to f8 means no mate.
  • 2.Bc4, 2.Nc3, anything odd: ...d5 anyway.
  • Queens off suits you here. Trade when offered, then play the structure.

KID-ish (Black vs everything else)

Compass: Nf6 first. Your pawn chain points where you attack. The g7 bishop is never for sale.

  • See c4? ...e5. No c4? ...c5.
  • London 7.c3: ...cxd4, ...Nc6, ...Qb6, then ...Nb4 hits the d3 bishop. Qb3? Qxb3. Knight kicks the queen? Qc7. b2 only when no knight can reach c4.
  • Their queen ate b7? Rb8 and chase her. Never offer the trade.
  • White plays d5 against ...e5: Ne8, f5, gxf5 with the pawn, f4, g5-g4. Ignore their queenside.
  • Bh6? Bh8. Never ...Bxc3 or ...Bxd4 for a pawn.
  • d5 hits your Nc6 (London): ...Ne5, recapture ...dxe5. Then a6, b5, Rb8.
  • e5 hits your Nf6: can you just take it? If not, Nfd7 and chip with ...c5 or ...f6.
  • Pawns trap, exchanges relieve. Tighten with a pawn instead of cashing in.

Endgame

Compass: Queens off? King on. Then push the passer.

  • Ahead: pieces off. Behind: pawns off.
  • Rooks behind passed pawns, yours or theirs.
  • Bare king? Before every quiet move, ask whether he has one.

Last lesson

Their queen ate b7? Rb8, chase her, never trade. A queen in your camp is out of play; a trade rescues her. (FPWgeBkK, 31 Oct 2026.) The one before: a pawn is a curtain too. It cost this game as well: 17...e5 moved d6 off the g3-c7 diagonal and the c7 rook fell.

Carefree smile. One lesson is all you need. Go.
